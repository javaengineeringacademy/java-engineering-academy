# Enterprise Legacy Technologies

## Overview

Enterprise legacy technologies represent the foundational systems that power critical business operations worldwide. These technologies, many originating from the 1980s and 1990s, continue to process trillions of dollars in transactions daily despite the rise of modern frameworks and platforms.

## Why Legacy Technologies Matter

Over 90% of global financial transactions still pass through COBOL-based mainframe systems. Government agencies, healthcare providers, airlines, and manufacturing companies depend on legacy infrastructure that has been refined over decades.

Key characteristics of legacy systems include proven reliability, massive data stores, deep integration with business processes, and significant institutional knowledge embedded in their codebases.

## Common Legacy Technologies

| Technology | Primary Use | Status |
|------------|------------|--------|
| COBOL | Banking, Insurance | Active Maintenance |
| Mainframe (z/OS) | Transaction Processing | Active Development |
| AS/400 (IBM i) | Mid-range Computing | Active Platform |
| Delphi | Desktop Applications | Niche Active |
| VB6 | Rapid Application Development | End of Life |
| Classic ASP | Web Applications | Legacy Support |

## Skills in Demand

Professionals with legacy technology skills command premium salaries due to scarcity. COBOL developers, mainframe specialists, and AS/400 programmers are among the highest-paid IT consultants in specific markets.

## Modernization Approaches

Organizations typically pursue one of four strategies: refactor, rehost, rewrite, or replace. The choice depends on business criticality, available expertise, budget constraints, and risk tolerance.

## Career Opportunities

Legacy technology expertise opens doors to consulting roles, modernization projects, and positions in industries resistant to change. Many Fortune 500 companies actively recruit for these skills.

## Module Structure

This module covers the most prevalent enterprise legacy technologies, their architectures, common use cases, and pathways for modernization and integration with contemporary systems.
