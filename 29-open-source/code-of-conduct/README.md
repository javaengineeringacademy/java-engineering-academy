## Introduction

A Code of Conduct (CoC) is a document that establishes expectations for behavior in a community. It helps create a welcoming, inclusive environment where everyone can participate safely and respectfully.

Every open source project should have a Code of Conduct. It signals that the project values its contributors and is committed to maintaining a positive community.

---

## Why Codes of Conduct Matter

### Benefits

1. **Creates Safe Spaces**
   - Defines acceptable behavior
   - Protects vulnerable community members
   - Encourages diverse participation

2. **Sets Expectations**
   - Clarifies what is and isn't acceptable
   - Provides guidelines for conflicts
   - Establishes consequences for violations

3. **Builds Trust**
   - Shows commitment to inclusivity
   - Demonstrates professionalism
   - Attracts diverse contributors

4. **Prevents Harassment**
   - Defines harassment clearly
   - Provides reporting mechanisms
   - Establishes enforcement procedures

### Impact on Community Health

Studies show that projects with Codes of Conduct:
- Have more diverse contributors
- Experience less toxicity
- Retain contributors longer
- Attract more first-time contributors

## Common Codes of Conduct

### Contributor Covenant

The most widely used Code of Conduct in open source.

**Key Points:**
- Pledge to make participation free from harassment
- Examples of positive and unacceptable behavior
- Clear enforcement guidelines
- Applied to all project spaces

**Website:** [www.contributor-covenant.org](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/)

### Citizen Code of Conduct

Focuses on creating positive community experiences.

**Key Points:**
- Values diversity and inclusion
- Addresses harassment explicitly
- Provides enforcement guidelines
- Emphasizes community impact

### Apache Code of Conduct

Used by Apache Software Foundation projects.

**Key Points:**
- Based on Contributor Covenant
- Adds Apache-specific guidelines
- Addresses ASF governance
- Provides reporting procedures

### Linux Foundation Code of Conduct

Used by Linux Foundation projects.

**Key Points:**
- Addresses harassment and discrimination
- Provides reporting mechanisms
- Establishes enforcement procedures
- Emphasizes inclusivity

## Key Principles

### 1. Respect

- Treat everyone with respect
- Value different perspectives
- Listen actively
- Be considerate of others

### 2. Inclusivity

- Welcome people of all backgrounds
- Use inclusive language
- Be mindful of accessibility
- Avoid assumptions

### 3. Professionalism

- Be professional in communications
- Focus on constructive feedback
- Avoid personal attacks
- Maintain boundaries

### 4. Safety

- Create safe spaces
- Protect vulnerable members
- Address harassment promptly
- Support victims

### 5. Accountability

- Take responsibility for actions
- Accept feedback gracefully
- Make amends when wrong
- Follow through on commitments

## Enforcement

### Enforcement Levels

#### Level 1: Warning
- Private warning to the individual
- Explain which behavior was inappropriate
- Set expectations for future behavior

#### Level 2: Temporary Ban
- Temporary removal from community spaces
- Duration depends on severity
- Clear path to return

#### Level 3: Permanent Ban
- Permanent removal from all project spaces
- For severe or repeated violations
- Last resort option

### Enforcement Guidelines

```markdown
## Enforcement Guidelines

### Level 1: Correction
- **Behavior**: Inappropriate language or actions
- **Response**: Private written warning
- **Consequences**: No further action if corrected

### Level 2: Warning
- **Behavior**: Continued inappropriate behavior
- **Response**: Public warning and temporary ban
- **Consequences**: Ban from community spaces

### Level 3: Temporary Ban
- **Behavior**: Serious violation or repeated offenses
- **Response**: Temporary removal from all spaces
- **Consequences**: Must meet with maintainers to return

### Level 4: Permanent Ban
- **Behavior**: Severe violation or sustained harassment
- **Response**: Permanent removal from all spaces
- **Consequences**: No return
```

### Enforcement Best Practices

1. **Be Consistent**: Apply rules equally to everyone
2. **Be Transparent**: Explain decisions clearly
3. **Be Fair**: Consider context and intent
4. **Be Confidential**: Protect reporter privacy
5. **Be Documented**: Keep records of actions taken

## Creating Your Own

### Template

```markdown
# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity
and orientation.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

* Using welcoming and inclusive language
* Being respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences
* Gracefully accepting constructive criticism
* Focusing on what is best for the community
* Showing empathy towards other community members

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or electronic
  address, without their explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of
acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in
response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive,
or harmful.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when
an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be
reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at
[INSERT CONTACT METHOD].

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining
the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed
unprofessional or unwelcome.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing
clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the
behavior was inappropriate.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series
of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No
interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with
those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community for a specified period of time.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within
the community.

## Attribution

This Code of Conduct is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant][homepage],
version 2.0, available at
https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/0/code_of_conduct.html.

Community Impact Guidelines were inspired by [Mozilla's code of conduct
enforcement ladder](https://github.com/mozilla/diversity).

[homepage]: https://www.contributor-covenant.org
```

### Customization Tips

1. **Add Contact Information**: Provide reporting channels
2. **Specify Scope**: Define where it applies
3. **Include Examples**: Add project-specific examples
4. **Update Regularly**: Keep it current
5. **Translate**: Make it accessible globally

## Reporting Issues

### Reporting Channels

- **Email**: Private reporting to maintainers
- **GitHub Issues**: For non-sensitive matters
- **Dedicated Form**: Anonymous reporting form
- **Designated Person**: Specific contact person

### What to Include

```markdown
## Report Template

### Incident Description
Describe what happened.

### People Involved
Who was involved?

### Evidence
Screenshots, links, or other evidence.

### Impact
How did this affect you or others?

### Desired Outcome
What would you like to happen?
```

### Reporting Best Practices

1. **Report Promptly**: Don't wait to report
2. **Be Specific**: Provide details and evidence
3. **Be Honest**: Don't exaggerate or minimize
4. **Be Confidential**: Don't publicize reports
5. **Follow Up**: Check on resolution

## Being an Ally

### How to Be an Ally

1. **Speak Up**: Address inappropriate behavior
2. **Listen**: Believe and support victims
3. **Educate**: Learn about different experiences
4. **Advocate**: Champion inclusive practices
5. **Model Behavior**: Set a positive example

### Allyship in Action

```markdown
## Examples of Allyship

### When You Witness Harassment
- Don't stay silent
- Document the incident
- Report it to maintainers
- Check in with the victim

### In Discussions
- Amplify underrepresented voices
- Challenge inappropriate language
- Welcome diverse perspectives
- Be mindful of your privilege

### In Code Reviews
- Focus on code, not the person
- Be constructive and kind
- Acknowledge good work
- Avoid assumptions
```

### Supporting Victims

1. **Believe Them**: Take reports seriously
2. **Listen**: Let them share their experience
3. **Validate**: Acknowledge their feelings
4. **Support**: Offer help and resources
5. **Follow Up**: Check on their wellbeing
